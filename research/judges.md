# GPT-6 Astra Hackathon NYC — Judge Dossiers

Research snapshot: 2026-09-10. The event page publicly names three judges “including” the people below, so this may not be the final or complete panel. The participant guide adds that an unnamed group of OpenAI judges screens the first-round submissions. No separate public presenter roster was found.

Note: the event page spells Sriram Krishnan's firm “Kearney Jackson.” The firm's own name is **Kearny Jackson**, which is used throughout this dossier.

## How the judges fit the official process

The official participant guide defines two rounds:

1. After the 5:30 p.m. deadline, an unnamed group of OpenAI judges reviews each submission and one-minute demo video. They select the five most interesting, useful, creative, and technically ambitious projects.
2. At 7:00 p.m., five finalists each give a three-minute live demo followed by two minutes of judge Q&A. Judges then choose the top three.

The public page names Klemmer, Krishnan, and Tecklu as members of a judging panel but does not assign them to a specific round. Confirm the final-stage panel onsite before tailoring any spoken material to an individual.

The official scoring rubric—not the personal dossiers—is the primary target:

| Criterion | Weight |
| --- | ---: |
| GPT-6 Astra in development | 25% |
| GPT-6 Astra in the finished project | 25% |
| Live demo | 25% |
| Technicality | 25% |

The profiles below are best used to anticipate Q&A and close calls between otherwise strong projects. They should not override the published rubric.

## Avery Klemmer — Thrive Capital

### Verified profile

Avery Klemmer is an investor at Thrive Capital in New York. A current Slush profile says she focuses on companies at both the infrastructure and application layers and is interested in teams that combine **technical expertise with product craft and creativity**. It associates her with ClickHouse, Databricks, Anduril, Rogo, Unify, A24, and The Browser Company. She holds a BA in Computer Science from Amherst College. Source: [Slush profile](https://slush.org/program/avery-klemmer).

Public career databases place her at FirstMark Capital before Thrive and describe earlier work at EY-Parthenon. The strongest directly relevant credential is her technical education plus investment work across software infrastructure, deep tech, and application products. Sources: [LinkedIn profile](https://www.linkedin.com/in/avery-klemmer) and [VCSift profile](https://www.vcsift.com/investors/profile/cb-avery-klemmer/). The latter appears to retain an old FirstMark bio, so use it only for career-history context.

### Public product and investment signals

- **Technical depth and product craft together.** Her Slush bio makes this unusually explicit; it is the clearest signal for a hackathon team.
- **Infrastructure and applications both count.** Her named company set ranges from data infrastructure (ClickHouse, Databricks) to defense/deep tech (Anduril), enterprise AI (Rogo), media (A24), and user-facing software (The Browser Company).
- **Novel AI interfaces are interesting.** In a 2023 Reuters interview, Klemmer argued that ChatGPT did not eliminate room for startups and expected novel formats and forms of engagement to emerge. Source: Reuters, [“OpenAI investors see more startup opportunities despite ChatGPT expansion”](https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/openai-investors-see-more-startup-opportunities-despite-chatgpt-expansion-3229822).
- **She is close to the OpenAI ecosystem.** Thrive is an OpenAI investor, and Klemmer has spoken publicly in that context. This raises the bar for merely demonstrating a model call; she is likely familiar with the baseline capability.

### Prior judging behavior

No reliable public record of Klemmer judging another hackathon or a published scorecard was found. That absence is meaningful: do not claim a known weighting or try to reverse-engineer her vote from an unrelated Thrive partner's behavior.

### Likely evaluation lens

The following is inference from her public bio and comments, not a disclosed rubric:

1. **Is there real technical substance?** A project should expose the hard part: orchestration, verification, interaction design, or systems work.
2. **Is the product crafted, not merely wired together?** The demo should feel inevitable and easy to understand.
3. **Does Astra enable a new interaction format?** A workflow that changes how people engage with software is more aligned than a generic chatbot.
4. **Could the idea matter at scale?** Thrive's portfolio context suggests that a narrow demo can still point toward a large technical or market platform.

### Best way to pitch to her

- Show the product immediately, then reveal the technical architecture only where it explains the magic.
- Make one interaction feel new and native to Astra.
- Pair technical novelty with a polished user experience.
- Avoid presenting raw model capability as the product; explain the proprietary workflow, feedback loop, data advantage, or distribution wedge.

### Questions to rehearse

- Why does this require Astra rather than a smaller model plus conventional code?
- What part of the experience is genuinely new?
- How do you know the output or action is correct?
- Is this a feature inside an incumbent, or the seed of an important standalone product?

### Confidence

- Identity and current role: **high**.
- Sector/product signals: **high**.
- Prior judging behavior: **low evidence; no documented record found**.
- Inferred lens: **medium**.

### Prior judging behavior

No reliable public hackathon scorecard or prior judge appearance was found for this Sriram Krishnan. Search results frequently surfaced the other person with the same name, so undocumented claims were excluded.

### Likely evaluation lens

The following is inference from his stated investment thesis and recent investment writing:

1. **Immediate time-to-value.** Can a user get a meaningful outcome in minutes, not after a long implementation?
2. **Pragmatic B2B pain.** Is the workflow common, expensive, and underserved?
3. **Early wedge with a large market.** A hackathon prototype can be small if the expansion path is obvious.
4. **Customer proof over abstract novelty.** Even a lightweight design-partner signal or quantified pain will strengthen the pitch.
5. **Founder/operator judgment.** Expect attention to what was actually built and what the team learned under time pressure.

### Best way to pitch to him

- Name the customer and the repeated workflow in the first sentence.
- Quantify current cost or delay and the demo's time-to-value.
- Show one full action loop, not a dashboard of hypothetical features.
- Explain the expansion path from a single workflow to a durable system of record, intelligence, or action.
- If possible, mention one customer conversation, real workflow artifact, or observed usage signal.

### Questions to rehearse

- Who pays, and what are they replacing?
- How quickly does a user reach value?
- Why is this a company rather than a feature?
- How does the initial wedge expand across the customer's workflow?
- What did you learn from a real or representative user today?

### Documented prior judging

#### Craft Ventures HackAIthon, 2023

Tecklu was a preliminary judge for a three-day AI hackathon. The published criteria were:

- **Creativity:** how interesting or unique the project and problem-solving approach were.
- **Problem space:** the importance of the problem, with practical AI/ML uses scoring higher.
- **Impact:** how well the project accomplished its claimed task, including testing and readiness for users.
- **Effective use of LLMs and prompts:** whether the project used interesting model capabilities.
- **Technical merit:** technical sophistication and justified complexity.

Source: [HackAIthon by Craft Ventures](https://hackaithon-by-craft-ventures.devpost.com/).

This is the best direct evidence of how she has judged a comparable event. It does not prove she will use the same rubric today, but all five dimensions map closely to the Astra event's build prompt.

#### H. Naylor Fitzhugh Conference finals, 2021

The Harvard Business School conference program lists Tecklu as a finals judge. The available program confirms the role but does not expose a detailed personal scorecard. Source: [2021 conference program](https://www.library.hbs.edu/hc/aasu/imgs/pdfs/AASU_2021_Conference_Program_Combined_v10.pdf).

### Her own pitch advice

In a 2021 TechCrunch session, Tecklu argued that a pitch should make an investor lean forward and want to do diligence. She favored a conventional, information-rich deck over gimmicky teaser formats, while recognizing the power of a product demo once the story and context are established. She also advised founders not to expose their most proprietary material in an initial deck and recommended a narrated recorded demo at the final partnership stage to avoid last-minute failure. Sources: [TechCrunch video](https://techcrunch.com/video/nailing-your-pitch/) and [session recap](https://techcrunch.com/2021/07/14/khoslas-adina-tecklu-breaks-down-how-to-nail-your-pitch/).

For a hackathon, the practical reading is: give enough context for the judge to understand the decision, then show the product working; have a clean recording as insurance.

### Likely evaluation lens

The following combines her documented 2023 rubric with her current official bio:

1. **Important problem, stated from the customer's perspective.**
2. **A creative solution that depends on the model's distinctive capability.**
3. **A working, tested result rather than a concept video.**
4. **Enough technical depth to be defensible, without complexity for its own sake.**
5. **A path from clever feature to durable product.**
6. **A structured, information-complete pitch.**

### Best way to pitch to her

- Start with the customer and the high-stakes job to be done.
- Make the “why now / why Astra” argument specific.
- Show tests, fallbacks, or evidence that the product accomplishes the claimed task.
- Explain the durable system, data, workflow, or distribution advantage behind the initial feature.
- Keep the story structured; do not rely on mystique or a vague teaser.

### Questions to rehearse

- How important is this problem, and for whom?
- What is the non-consensus insight?
- Does the product accomplish the task reliably enough for a user?
- What is technically difficult or defensible here?
- What prevents an incumbent or the model provider from absorbing this as a feature?
- What would you test next to prove product-market fit?

## Panel-level synthesis

The official four-part rubric should drive build allocation. The panel-level observations below are secondary: they help shape the problem choice, product story, and likely Q&A once the entry can already demonstrate Astra-native development, Astra-native runtime value, a compelling live demo, and sound engineering.

### Where the three judges overlap

The panel is likely to reward the intersection of four qualities:

1. **Real technical leverage.** Astra should be essential to the workflow.
2. **Product clarity.** The audience should understand the transformation without a long explanation.
3. **Practical value.** A specific user should get a large, fast improvement.
4. **Company-shaped ambition.** The demo can be narrow, but the idea should not collapse into a single replaceable feature.

### Product categories with natural panel fit

This is not a recommendation to copy their portfolios. It is a map of areas where the panel already has relevant context:

- AI-native enterprise workflows and agents.
- Data, developer, and infrastructure tooling.
- Fintech and regulated operational workflows.
- Tools that turn expert or institutional knowledge into action.
- Novel AI interaction models with strong product craft.
- Vertical software where one workflow provides an expansion wedge.

### What may underperform

- A generic chat interface over a small document set.
- A broad “AI assistant for everyone” without a concrete user or job.
- A technically ornate agent swarm without visible user value.
- A polished mockup that cannot complete the claimed task.
- An impressive model demo with no durable product thesis.
- A pitch that overclaims autonomy but cannot show verification or failure handling.

## Recommended panel-aware close

A strong final sentence would combine all three lenses:

> “For [specific user], Astra turns [expensive, repeated workflow] from [old cost/time] into [verified outcome] in [new cost/time]; today's demo proves the wedge, and the same action-and-evidence layer expands into [larger durable product].”
