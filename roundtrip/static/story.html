What if client feedback went straight into Lightroom?

Imagine sending a portrait gallery and getting a request for cooler skin tones and a darker background. An assistant makes those changes in your original RAW edit, exports a new version, and returns it for review. You can inspect or undo every adjustment.

This demo tests the editing part: I used a reference portrait, edited a RAW in Lightroom, reviewed the export, and made a second version from my own feedback.

The reference

What I took from it

In the reference, warm skin stands out against a darker, less colorful background. The dark hair has depth, while the white shirt retains detail. I used those relationships to guide your edit.

I reduced blue and purple saturation, lowered highlights, deepened blacks, and added grain. The second revision darkens the background separately so the face draws more attention.

How it relates to the recipe

Mullins uses Classic Chrome, a warmer white-balance shift, reduced color, softer highlights, stronger shadows, and weak grain. Those choices informed the direction; the Fujifilm values do not translate directly to Lightroom.

Your ORF uses Adobe Color. Its lavender wall, lighting, and composition are different, so this is an interpretation of the reference rather than an exact match.

First edit

I warmed the white balance to 6000 K, reduced blue and purple saturation, lowered highlights, and added grain. This produced V1.

Feedback on V1

“The bright wall still competes with her face, and the skin is a little too golden.”

I reviewed the exported JPEG before making another edit.

Second edit

I changed the white balance to 5700 K with +2 tint and lowered the background exposure by 0.55 stops using a Lightroom mask. The framing and facial details remain the same.
